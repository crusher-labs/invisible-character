What an invisible character is

An invisible character is a real Unicode character with no visible glyph. To you it looks like nothing; to an app it is genuine text that can be sent, saved and counted. That difference is what makes blank usernames, empty messages and invisible spacing possible on platforms that reject plain spaces.

The big blue button copies the Hangul filler (U+3164), which works in the most places: WhatsApp blank messages, Discord and Fortnite-style empty names, Instagram line spacing, form fields that demand "something". The table gives the other blank characters (zero-width space, braille blank, word joiner and more) for platforms that filter specific ones, and the generator produces any amount of invisible text at once.

Why does my blank username get rejected?

Some platforms filter specific invisible characters. Try another one from the table: the braille blank (U+2800) often passes where the Hangul filler is blocked, and zero-width characters work where any visible width is rejected.

What is the difference between a zero-width and a blank character?

A blank character (Hangul filler, braille blank) takes up visible width, like a space that platforms cannot strip. A zero-width character (U+200B, U+200C, U+200D) has no width at all: useful for invisible separators or sneaking past minimum-length rules without any visible gap.

Is this safe to use?

They are ordinary Unicode characters, the same ones used legitimately in Korean text and complex scripts. Nothing is exploited. Just avoid using them to impersonate someone else's blank-looking name.
